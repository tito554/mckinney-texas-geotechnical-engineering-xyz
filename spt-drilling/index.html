McKinney sits on a tricky mix of Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford Shale, and pockets of highly expansive Taylor Marl. The water table here can swing from 15 to 30 feet depending on the season and proximity to the Trinity River tributaries. Before you drop a slab or drill a pier, you need to know exactly what that soil profile looks like at depth. A standard penetration test gives you hard numbers, not guesses. Our team runs the SPT drill rig across Collin County every week, from the limestone benches near the airport to the clay-heavy lots east of Highway 75. We pull samples, count blows, and log the recovery so your structural engineer can design with confidence. For sites where the refusal hits shallow rock, we often pair the SPT with a CPT test to fill in the soft soil profile before the auger kicks out on limestone.

N-values under 4 in the upper 8 feet of McKinney clay mean you are dealing with active soils. That number changes everything about your foundation design.

Process and scope

In McKinney, we consistently see N-values jump from single digits in the upper weathered clay to refusal at 15 or 20 feet when you hit competent chalk. That transition zone is where most foundation problems start. The SPT procedure follows ASTM D1586: we drive a 2-inch split spoon sampler 18 inches with a 140-pound hammer dropping 30 inches, recording the blow count for each 6-inch increment. The standard N-value is the sum of the second and third increments. It is simple, brutal, and effective. We run the test at 2.5-foot intervals in the upper 10 feet, then every 5 feet until termination. The data feeds directly into bearing capacity calculations, settlement estimates, and liquefaction screening per ASCE 7. When the clay layers show high plasticity, we send samples to the lab for Atterberg limits to correlate the N-values with the actual swell potential.

Local geotechnical context

The seasonal moisture swings in North Texas wreak havoc on expansive clays. Summers bake the soil to shrinkage cracks; spring rains send it swelling again. If you skip the SPT and just assume a generic 2000 psf bearing pressure, you are gambling. We have seen slabs in McKinney move two inches in a single drought cycle because the soil report missed a thin layer of fat clay at 6 feet. The SPT catches that layer. Every time. Plus, with the area lying in Seismic Design Category A per IBC, the dynamic loading is not your main worry here, but the expansive soil behavior is. The blow count log tells you where the active zone ends, which directly dictates your pier depth or whether you need to over-excavate and bring in select fill. In the eastern sections of the city, where the Eagle Ford weathers to a slick, blocky clay, we recommend sampling every 2.5 feet through the entire weathered profile.

Quick answers

What does an SPT test cost for a single-family lot in McKinney?

For a standard residential lot, one borehole with SPT sampling every 2.5 feet typically runs between US$620 and US$870. That includes mobilization, drilling, sampling, and the geotechnical report. If the lot has difficult access or we need a second boring to meet IBC requirements, the cost adjusts accordingly.

How deep do you typically drill for a slab foundation in McKinney?

We usually go to 20 or 25 feet, or until we hit refusal on the Austin Chalk. The goal is to penetrate completely through the weathered, expansive clay zone and into competent material. The IBC requires borings to extend through all unsuitable layers, so depth varies by neighborhood.

Do you need SPT testing if the lot already has a soils report from the 1990s?

Almost always yes. The City of McKinney building department generally will not accept a report older than five years for new construction. Soil conditions change with adjacent development, drainage modifications, and extended drought cycles. A new SPT program gives you current data that reflects today's site conditions.

How long does the drilling and the report take?

Drilling one borehole takes about half a day on site. We deliver the geotechnical report within five to seven business days after fieldwork. If you need it faster for a permit deadline, we can expedite the lab testing and reporting.
